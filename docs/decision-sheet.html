The game

One paragraph

Bursa Tales is a co-op first-person shooter with survival, base-building and tower-defense elements, for up to 8 players on a persistent dedicated server you run yourselves. A mysterious event has teleported your old high-school bursa on Okopowa Street in Warsaw into a psychedelic hell. You play its former residents: you fight demons Doom-style with a growing arsenal of weird weapons, gather resources and craft better gear, fortify the bursa and hold it against demon attacks, and find your old dorm-mates, now NPCs who give quests, until you are strong enough to kill the boss and escape. It should feel tense, psychedelic and creative, look low-poly and stylized (Schedule I rather than realistic), run on Windows and macOS, and leave voice chat to Discord. The first version is deliberately tiny: the bursa map, players, demons, weapons and shooting, all in multiplayer. Crafting, building, tower defense, NPCs, quests, story and skills come later.

What’s realistic

Your Section 9 question

You have 2 × 8 hours a week, about 16 hours together, or 65–70 hours a month. First-game estimates usually run two to three times over, so every step below is small and ends with something you can play.

v0.1About 6–8 weeks

  • 2–8 players on your own dedicated server
  • Graybox bursa: ground floor, a corridor, the courtyard
  • Two weapons against waves of one demon type
  • Health, death, respawn, a wave counter
  • “A few weekends” is the optimistic end

NextAbout 6 months

  • Call of Duty Zombies loop: demon currency and a mystery vending machine
  • Barricades and turrets (tower defense lite)
  • 4–6 weapons, 3–4 demon types, a first boss
  • World saves on a VPS with automatic builds

LaterAbout 12 months

  • Simple crafting and free-placement base building that persists
  • A few NPC friends with simple quests
  • Sanity with psychedelic effects
  • A second zone: the streets around the bursa, or the mall

Not realistic for a first game

  • Writing your own engine or physics
  • Factorio-style automation, RimWorld-style NPC lives, true RTS unit control: each is a whole game
  • A detailed replica of Okopowa and Arkadia; build a compressed, stylized version
  • Hundreds of networked demons at once; plan for 20–50
  • Fair competitive PvP, anti-cheat, accounts, matchmaking, a Steam release
  • Making all 3D art and animation yourselves, or a story with cutscenes and voice acting

Biggest risks

  • Scope. The brief touches about ten genres. Add one new system per milestone, and only once the current build is fun.
  • The multiplayer tax. Every feature costs more over the network. Building it in from day one, as you plan, is still cheaper than adding it later.
  • Motivation. Projects on 8-hour weeks die in long stretches with nothing playable, so every milestone ends with the two of you playing.
  • The physics/network split. Player movement belongs to both of you, so CLAUDE.md will define the interface between your areas.

  8. 8.2 gives Player 1 the “game engine”, but with an existing engine there is no engine to write. D13 D17
